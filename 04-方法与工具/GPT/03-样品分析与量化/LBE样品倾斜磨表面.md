> Lead-bismuth eutectic (LBE, Pb44.5Bi55.5, wt. %) has emerged as a primary candidate coolant and spallation target material for Generation IV lead-cooled fast reactors (LFRs) and accelerator-driven systems (ADS), owing to its exceptional neutron economy, thermohydraulic properties, and chemical stability at elevated temperatures [1–5]. The low melting point (123.5 ◦C), high boiling point (1670 ◦C) and low vapor pressure of LBE impart enhanced thermal efficiency and inherent safety margin to reactor when operating above 500 ◦C [1,3]. However, the practical implementation of LBE-cooled systems faces critical challenges in LBE-materials compatibility, particularly liquid metal corrosion (LMC) and liquid metal embrittlement (LME) of structural materials [1,2,5,6]. The primary alloying elements (Fe, Cr, Ni) of two candidate structural materials (ferritic/martensitic (F/M) steel and austenitic stainless steel (AuSS)) for LFRs exhibit high solubility in LBE, particularly the austenite-stabilizing element Ni, which demonstrates solubility several orders of magnitude higher than Fe and Cr [5,6]. Moreover, the solubility increases significantly with rising temperature [6]. Consequently, when unprotected steel surfaces are directly exposed to LBE under elevated operating temperatures (>450 ◦C for AuSSs and >550 ◦C for F/M steels), severe dissolution occurs [1]. Dissolution induces accelerated metal recession and austenite-to-ferrite phase transformation, progressively compromising structural integrity [7–11]. Furthermore, direct contact between LBE and structural materials may also induce LME, causing reduction in toughness or ductility [1,12,13]. The current strategy to mitigate dissolution corrosion and LME involves controlling the dissolved oxygen concentration in liquid LBE to form a protective oxide scale on the material surface, thereby isolating the metallic substrate from the corrosive LBE environment [14]. However, excessive oxide thickness not only compromises the load-bearing capacity of materials, but also significantly impairs the heat transfer efficiency of thermal exchange components such as fuel cladding and heat exchange tubes [1].
>
> Compared to F/M steels, AuSSs exhibit superior high-temperature mechanical properties, enhanced oxidation resistance, and reduced susceptibility to LME, making them the main candidate structural materials for out-of-core components in LFRs [1,5,12,15]. However, the high solubility of Ni in LBE renders that AuSSs operate within oxygen-controlled environments [14]. Extensive investigations on the oxidation behavior of AuSSs in oxygen-containing LBE have been systematically conducted over recent decades [9,16–31]. It has been widely reported that AuSSs frequently exhibit preferential intergranular oxidation (PIO) in LBE [16–20,23,25,26,28–32]. PIO has also been extensively documented in AuSSs and Ni-based alloys exposed to high temperature water [33–38], high temperature steam [39–41], supercritical water (SCW) [42–44], and supercritical carbon dioxide (S-CO2) or high temperature CO2 [45–47], with increasing studies confirming the close correlation between PIO and stress corrosion cracking (SCC) in these environments [33,40,43,48–51]. As for LBE environments, the intergranular oxidation may also induce preferential cracking along oxidized grain boundaries (GBs). Investigation on low-cycle fatigue behavior of T91 steel in oxygen-saturated LBE (150–550 ◦C) by Xue et al. [52] revealed that enhanced oxidation at 550 ◦C degraded matrix strength while increasing crack initiation sites. Wang et al. [32] found that most cracks, formed on 316Ti steel under tensile stress in oxygen-containing LBE at 550 ◦C, stopped at the oxide/matrix interface, while deeper cracks propagating along oxidized GBs into the metal matrix. Notably, Wang et al. [25] observed pronounced intergranular cracking along preferentially oxidized GBs in 15–15Ti steel exposed to LBE at 550 ◦C without external stress, accompanied by Pb infiltration into the cracks. This indicates that dissolution induced by Pb penetration along oxidized GBs can directly lead to intergranular cracking. These preferentially oxidized GBs would serve as preferential cracking paths, triggering premature failure. Therefore, the degradation in loading capacity of AuSSs in LBE environments is closely related to the PIO behavior which should be carefully studied.
>
> Several investigations have been conducted on the PIO behavior of austenitic steels in oxygen-containing LBE [19,23,25,28,29,31]. It has been revealed that the oxidized GBs are decorated with Fe-Cr spinel [28,29,31]. Nano-network structured IOZ (internal oxidation zone) was observed on the flanks of intergranular oxides [19,23,28,29,31]. Unoxidized metallic particles rich in Ni and depleted in Cr, termed Ni-enriched grains (NiEGs), were frequently observed either wrapped by intergranular oxides or on their periphery [23,25,28,29,31]. Nanoscale pores, which are believed to be spatially interconnected and act as the fast diffusion pathway during the oxidation process [19,26,27,53–59], were also distributed between intergranular oxides [19,23,28]. Additionally, some elongated pores were observed along the oxidized GBs [23,28]. Notably, the Ni-enriched and Cr-depleted diffusion induced grain boundary migration (DIGM) zones were observed at the PIO fronts in 15–15Ti steel exposed to oxygen-saturated LBE at 550 ◦C by Lu et al. [29]. >

The high solubility of the major alloying elements (Ni, Cr, Fe) of AuSSs in LBE makes LBE highly aggressive to these alloys. The microstructures of the surface and intergranular oxides can be degraded by LBE through dissolution and the oxidation would be aggravated. Currently, the microstructures of intergranular oxide in AuSSs exposed to LBE and the effect of LBE remains unclear. Systematic investigations into both the PIO microstructure and the formation mechanisms of intergranular pores and Ni-enriched phases are still needed to better understand the PIO behavior in LBE environment. The present work systematically investigated the intergranular oxidation behavior of 316L SS in oxygen-saturated LBE at 550 ◦C. Detailed microstructure characterization was conducted on the PIO fronts of two types of GB. A mechanism for intergranular oxidation of 316L SS exposed to LBE was proposed.

# LBE 腐蚀样品 XRD 实际上机指南

## 0. 先定目标
先判断您这次想看的到底是哪一类信息：

- **看表面氧化层**：优先 **GI-XRD / GIXRD**
- **看是否已经形成较厚的相变层（如 ferritic layer）**：先做 **常规 θ–2θ**
- **两者都想看**：先常规扫一遍，再做 GI-XRD

原因很简单：常规 XRD 采到的是**表面以下几微米**甚至更深的信息，而 GIXRD 用低入射角固定照射，更适合压低基体信号、强调表面薄层；而且多角度 GIXRD 还能做近似“深度分层”对比。([马尔文帕纳利提克尔](https://www.malvernpanalytical.com/en/products/technology/xray-analysis/x-ray-diffraction/grazing-incidence-x-ray-diffraction))

## 1. 样品前处理：怎么洗，洗到什么程度停

### 推荐原则
**只去掉“残余金属态 Pb-Bi”，不要再机械重抛腐蚀表面。**  
因为您一旦再磨表面，最想测的那层薄氧化物就没了。

### 可执行流程
先用**温和方法**去掉附着的 LBE，再上 XRD：

1. **低温加热去大块 LBE**
   - 可参考一份 316L/LBE 样品的做法：在 **135 °C 甘油中加热约 10 min**，使 LBE 重新熔化，再擦去大部分金属残留。([Roma](https://roma.sckcen.be/ws/portalfiles/portal/7413459/Microstructural_Investigation_of_316L_Stainless_Steel_in_LBE_Environment.pdf))
2. **再做短时间化学清洗**
   - 同一份样品制备里，用的是 **H2O2 : CH3COOH : H2O = 1:1:1**，配合超声，随后用乙醇清洗。([Roma](https://roma.sckcen.be/ws/portalfiles/portal/7413459/Microstructural_Investigation_of_316L_Stainless_Steel_in_LBE_Environment.pdf))
3. **最后只吹干，不再抛光**
   - 乙醇漂洗后，氮气吹干或洁净空气吹干即可。

### 一个重要提醒
有 LBE 腐蚀研究提到，样品表面若用了 **silicone oil** 之类辅助清理，而后续去除不彻底，EDS/XRD 这类表征会被 **Si/O 残留**干扰。  
所以您的目标是做 XRD 时，**尽量别用会留膜的清洗介质**；如果用了，一定确认已经彻底去净。([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2412-382X/5/3/27))

## 3. 几何模式怎么选

## 方案 A：您最该用的
### **GI-XRD / GIXRD**
这最适合 LBE 腐蚀后的**表面氧化层、超薄腐蚀层、局部复合氧化物**。

### **常规 θ–2θ**
适合先快速判断：

- 有没有明显氧化物峰
- 有没有 **α-ferrite / ferritic layer** 这种较厚相变层
- 样品整体背景和信噪比怎么样

对 316L 来说，低氧 LBE 下确实可能优先形成 **ferritic layer**；而氧含量足够高时，又更可能看到 **Fe3O4 + FeCr2O4** 一类氧化层。([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0010938X11002538))

## 4. 我建议您直接照着跑的扫描流程

下面给您一套**最稳、最省机时、又比较不容易漏相**的流程。

---

## 第一步：常规 θ–2θ 快速摸底
先看整体有没有东西。

### 参数
- 模式：**θ–2θ**
- 靶材：**Co Kα 优先**；没有就 Cu Kα
- 扫描范围：**20°–100° 2θ**
- 步长：**0.03°**
- 每步时间：**1 s/step**
- 总时长：大约 **40–50 min**

### 目的
这一步主要看三件事：

1. 是否已经能看到明显氧化物峰  
2. 是否基体峰太强，把表层信号完全盖住  
3. 是否出现了 ferritic layer 的迹象

### 结果判断
- **如果这一步已经能看到比较清楚的氧化物峰**：继续做精扫确认
- **如果几乎只有基体峰**：马上转 GI-XRD

---

## 第二步：GI-XRD，先扫最表层
### 参数
- 模式：**GI-XRD**
- 固定入射角 ω：**0.5°**
- 扫描范围：**20°–90° 2θ**
- 步长：**0.03°**
- 每步时间：**1 s/step**
- 总时长：大约 **35–45 min**

### 这一步看什么
这一步是专门抓**最表层**的：

- 外层 **Fe3O4**
- 很薄的 **Fe-Cr spinel / FeCr2O4**
- 某些表面富集的 **Pb-Fe-O** 复杂氧化物
- 很薄但连续的表层覆盖物

GIXRD 本来就是为了表面薄层和弱信号层而设的；低角度时可以显著压低基体贡献。([马尔文帕纳利提克尔](https://www.malvernpanalytical.com/en/products/technology/xray-analysis/x-ray-diffraction/grazing-incidence-x-ray-diffraction))
---

## 第三步：GI-XRD，再扫一次更深一点
### 参数
- 模式：**GI-XRD**
- 固定入射角 ω：**1.0°**
- 扫描范围：**20°–90° 2θ**
- 步长：**0.03°**
- 每步时间：**1 s/step**
- 总时长：大约 **35–45 min**

### 这一步的意义
把 **0.5°** 和 **1.0°** 两张谱一对比，您大概就能判断：

- 某个峰更偏外层还是偏里层
- 是单纯表面污染/残留，还是确实来自腐蚀层
- 是否存在“外层 magnetite、内层 spinel”这类分层趋势

多入射角 GIXRD 可用于比较不同深度层的信息，这是该方法的标准优势之一。([马尔文帕纳利提克尔](https://www.malvernpanalytical.com/en/products/technology/xray-analysis/x-ray-diffraction/grazing-incidence-x-ray-diffraction))
---

## 第四步：如果信号还是太弱，再加一遍 2° 或 3°
### 参数
- ω：**2.0°**
- 仍不够时再做 **3.0°**
- 其余参数不变

### 什么时候需要
只在两种情况下加：

- 腐蚀层并不特别薄，可能已经到 **亚微米到数微米**
- 0.5° / 1.0° 信号太弱，峰形不稳定

教学资料里给出的 GIXRD 入射角通常是小于 3° 的低角度范围；因此 **0.5° → 1.0° → 2.0°/3.0°** 这个阶梯式扫描，对您这种腐蚀层样品很实用。([Aalto University Wiki](https://wiki.aalto.fi/spaces/SSC/pages/119603863/Grazing%2Bincidence%2BXRD))
---

## 第五步：做一段精扫确认
不要全谱都慢扫，**只对关键窗口慢扫**。

### 参数
- 模式：选您刚才信号最好的一张谱对应的模式
- 扫描范围：**25°–75° 2θ**
- 步长：**0.02°**
- 每步时间：**3 s/step**
- 总时长：大约 **2 小时左右**

### 为什么这样做
您关心的相多数会落在这个区间内，而且这一步的作用是：

- 分清弱峰是不是噪声
- 看清 shoulder / 峰重叠
- 给后面软件检索和拟合留更好的数据

# 6. 上机后，您大概率会看到什么

## 如果是高氧/控氧 LBE
316L 在足够高氧浓度或较长腐蚀时间下，文献常见的是：

- **Fe3O4（magnetite）**
- **FeCr2O4 / Fe-Cr spinel**
